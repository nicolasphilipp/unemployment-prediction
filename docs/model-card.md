# Model Card: Random Forest Unemployment Predictor v1.0

## Model Description
This model is a Random Forest regression pipeline designed to predict the average annual number of unemployed persons (aged 15-64) across the districts of Vienna. It utilizes a combination of a standard scaler and a random forest regressor implemented via scikit-learn. The primary predictive features include guest overnight stays (tourism), average district population, and one-hot-encoded district codes. Hyperparameters such as the number of estimators and tree depth were optimized using an exhaustive grid search approach on a dedicated validation dataset.

## Intended Use
The intended use of this Random Forest model is primarily for research and educational purposes within the domain of urban socio-economic analysis. It serves as an analytical tool to explore the historical relationship between tourism activity and local employment levels in Vienna. Furthermore, researchers can utilize this model as a baseline architecture for developing more complex, multivariate socio-economic forecasting pipelines.

## Out-of-Scope Uses
This model is strictly not intended for deployment in active public policy formulation or administrative decision-making processes. It should not be utilized to rank, penalize, or financially evaluate specific city districts, as the feature set is highly constrained. Applying this model to predict unemployment during severe economic anomalies, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, is also out of scope since data from 2020 and 2021 were explicitly excluded during training.

## Training Data
The model was trained on the 'TU Wien DBRepo - Unemployment and Tourism Experiment Database', which integrates official open government data from the City of Vienna. The combined training and validation dataset covers the years 2002 to 2018 (excluding pandemic years) and consists of 391 distinct district-year observations. The primary source database storing this training data can be persistently accessed and cited using its Digital Object Identifier (DOI): `doi:10.82556/n73d-ks38`.

## Evaluation Results
The model was evaluated against a chronologically held-out test set comprising 69 observations from the years 2019, 2022, and 2023. The evaluation indicates that the model performs robustly for low-to-medium unemployment values, capturing the general variance effectively. However, it systematically underestimates the unemployment levels in districts with exceptionally high structural unemployment, indicating that additional socio-economic variables are necessary for perfect prediction. ## Limitations
One of the primary limitations of this model is its reliance on only two main numeric predictors, entirely ignoring crucial factors like education, industry composition, and housing costs. Additionally, the use of one-hot-encoded district identifiers restricts the model's transferability, meaning it cannot be applied to newly formed districts or other cities without complete retraining. Finally, because the model was evaluated using a single chronological train-test split rather than cross-validation, its performance metrics may be somewhat sensitive to the specific years chosen for testing.

## Ethical Considerations
Unemployment is a highly sensitive socio-economic indicator that directly impacts human livelihoods and community reputations. Consequently, predictions generated by this model should never be used in public-facing applications without extensive and clear contextualization regarding its limitations. Users must also strictly remember that the model merely captures historical correlations between tourism and unemployment; it absolutely does not establish any causal link between overnight stays and job losses or gains.
